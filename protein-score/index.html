How the score works

A low score doesn't make a food "bad". It tells you how well it does the job of a protein source, so you can compare similar products.

The four factors
  • Protein density (30%): the share of calories that come from protein. 10% or less scores zero, 60% or more scores full marks.
  • Protein quality (35%): based on the DIAAS score of the main protein source, which measures how well its essential amino acids are digested and used. Anything at 1.0 or above gets full marks.
  • Carb efficiency (20%): grams of protein per gram of net carbohydrate (carbs minus half of any polyols). EU labels already leave fibre out of carbohydrate, so we don't subtract it again.
  • Sugar ratio (15%): protein ÷ (protein + sugar). Products where sugar rivals protein drop fast.

If you choose "Not sure" for the source, the score uses the other three factors only.

Why per 100g, and why fat isn't scored

EU labels always show per-100g values, so scoring on them lets you compare a bar with a yogurt fairly, whatever the serving size. Fat isn't penalised: this rates protein sources, not overall healthiness. Calories from fat still lower protein density.

Leucine and "usable protein"

Leucine is the amino acid that switches on muscle building. Around 2.5g per meal or snack is a commonly cited target for a strong response. We estimate it from the protein per serving and the typical leucine content of the source. It isn't part of the score because it depends on how much you eat.

Usable protein is protein per 100g multiplied by the source's DIAAS (capped at 1.0). It's a rough guide to how much of the label figure your body can put to work. That's why collagen-based products can claim lots of protein yet show almost none here.
