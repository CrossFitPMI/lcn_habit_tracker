Your 12 weeks

How it works

Every calculator gives an estimate. Use these numbers for 2–3 weeks, watch your weekly average weight, and adjust by 100–200 kcal if you need to.

How your calories are worked out

We estimate the calories you burn at rest using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, or the Katch-McArdle equation if you enter your body fat. We then add your daily movement and training to get your maintenance calories, the amount that keeps your weight steady.

To lose fat we take away enough to lose about 0.5% (steady) or 0.75% (faster) of your body weight a week. To build muscle we add enough for about 0.25% or 0.5% a week.

Safety limits

We never cut more than 25% below your maintenance calories, and we never go below your resting energy needs (or 1,200 kcal for women and 1,500 kcal for men, whichever is higher). If you're already at a low body weight, we won't suggest losing more.

This calculator is for healthy adults. It isn't suitable during pregnancy or breastfeeding, for under-18s, or if you have a medical condition or a history of disordered eating. In those cases please talk to your GP or a registered dietitian.

Why protein is set by body weight

Protein is the one target that matters most for keeping and building muscle, so we set it from your body weight rather than as a percentage of calories: about 1.8 g per kg to maintain or build, and 2.0 g per kg while losing fat. Choose High protein and these go up to 2.0 and 2.2 g per kg, with carbs coming down to make room. If your BMI is above 30 and you haven't entered your body fat, we base it on a healthy reference weight for your height so the target stays realistic.

How the hand portions work

Hand portions are a simple way to eat to your targets without weighing or tracking. A palm of protein is roughly 20–25 g of protein, a cupped hand of carbs roughly 25–30 g of carbs, and a thumb of fats roughly 8–10 g of fat. They're approximate, and that's fine. Consistency matters far more than precision.
